Apple: look beyond the familiar products.

Apple combines hardware, software, services, and a tightly integrated ecosystem. A familiar brand is a starting point—not a valuation.

What the business depends on

Research the contribution of iPhone, Mac, iPad, wearables, and services across several reporting periods. Services may have different margins and recurring characteristics from hardware, while device demand can depend on replacement cycles and consumer confidence.

Evidence worth tracking

  • Revenue growth by product and geography.
  • Gross margin, operating margin, and operating cash flow.
  • Installed-device base and services growth.
  • Research spending, capital expenditure, and supplier commitments.
  • Share repurchases compared with employee share issuance.

Risks the chart cannot measure

Important questions include supply-chain concentration, dependence on flagship products, platform regulation, app-distribution rules, competition, currency movements, and the expectations already embedded in the share price.

Keep three conclusions separate.

AAPL can have a positive price trend while appearing expensive, or a weak trend while the underlying business remains profitable. Price, quality, and valuation need independent evidence.

Primary sources

Begin with Apple's latest annual report, quarterly reports, earnings release, and investor-relations material. Check reporting dates and units rather than relying on isolated figures repeated by third parties.
