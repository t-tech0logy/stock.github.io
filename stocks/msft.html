Microsoft: recurring software meets heavy infrastructure.

Microsoft spans workplace software, cloud computing, cybersecurity, gaming, search, and AI. The parts grow and consume capital differently.

What the business depends on

Separate commercial subscriptions, Azure and server products, Windows-related revenue, gaming, search, and devices. Enterprise contracts can support recurring revenue, while cloud and AI expansion require large data-centre investment.

Evidence worth tracking

  • Revenue and operating income by reported segment.
  • Cloud growth and whether growth is accelerating or slowing.
  • Capital expenditure and operating cash flow.
  • Remaining performance obligations and customer commitments.
  • Margins after depreciation and AI infrastructure costs.

Risks the chart cannot measure

Competition in cloud and AI, antitrust action, cybersecurity failures, execution across acquisitions, customer optimization, and rising infrastructure costs can change the business case before a moving average reacts.

Growth has a cost.

AI-related demand can lift revenue while simultaneously requiring unusually high spending. Compare cash generated with the capital required to sustain that growth.

Primary sources

Use Microsoft's annual report, quarterly filings, earnings materials, and segment disclosures. Keep fiscal periods consistent when comparing growth rates.
