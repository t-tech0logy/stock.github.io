1. Recent price trend · 25%

Measures approximately one- and three-month price changes. Stronger sustained movement receives a higher score than falling or indecisive movement.

2. Longer price trend · 25%

Compares the latest price with its 50-day and 200-day averages. These averages smooth daily noise, but they react after the market has already moved.

3. Wider-market comparison · 20%

Compares the investment with the S&P 500 over the available period. Outperformance can show relative strength; underperformance can show that capital has found stronger alternatives elsewhere.

4. Trading activity · 10%

Checks whether recent volume supports the direction of the move. Volume can add context, but it does not explain why people traded.

5. Yearly range position · 10%

Locates the current price between its approximate one-year low and high. This describes positioning, not whether a stock is fundamentally cheap or expensive.

6. Stability · 10%

Uses variability and drawdown to describe how bumpy the price journey has been. Historical stability does not guarantee future stability.

How to read the result

  • 70–100: Positive price trend. Several price measures are currently supportive.
  • 50–69: Mixed price trend. Evidence is divided, incomplete, or not yet convincing.
  • Below 50: Weak price trend. Price direction or stability currently warrants caution.
What the score does not include

Revenue, profit, cash flow, debt, competitive position, management quality, valuation, tax, portfolio diversification, and your personal financial circumstances.

Data quality and timing

The dashboard uses the latest data returned by its configured provider. Prices may be delayed, incomplete, adjusted, or corrected later. A missing component reduces confidence rather than being treated as positive evidence.

Known limitations

Any fixed scoring model can oversimplify changing markets. Trend measures can reverse quickly, moving averages lag, and a comparison with the S&P 500 may be unsuitable for some international securities or asset classes. The output should begin research, not end it.
