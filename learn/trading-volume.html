Trading activity

Volume shows participation, not intention.

Trading volume counts how many shares changed hands. It can show that a move attracted attention, but it cannot identify every trader's reason.

Why investors watch volume

Volume is the number of shares traded. High volume means more people or institutions took part than usual.

Participation changes from day to day

What this shows: taller bars mean more shares changed hands. The tallest bar marks unusually high participation—not whether buyers or sellers were correct.

Every trade has two sides

Every completed trade has a buyer and a seller. Volume shows activity, but it does not tell us why each person traded.

Context changes the interpretation.

High volume during a rise may support the move. High volume during a fall may show fear. The number alone cannot explain the story.

Use the stock's own normal level

Compare today's volume with that same stock's usual volume. Comparing Apple directly with a small company would not be useful.

How PlainStock uses volume

Trading activity contributes 10% of the score. It supports the other evidence but never decides the result by itself.
