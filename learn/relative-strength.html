Market comparison

A rising stock can still be falling behind.

Relative strength compares two investments over the same period. It asks whether the stock rewarded its holders more or less than a useful alternative.

The basic calculation

Relative strength means comparing a stock with the wider market. If a stock gained 8% while the market gained 12%, the stock went up but still fell behind by four percentage points.

Same period, different result
Market+12%
Stock+8%
Stock +8%−Market +12%=4 points behind

What this shows: both investments rose, but choosing the stock produced four percentage points less than the market.

Why the benchmark matters

A benchmark is the comparison group. PlainStock normally uses the S&P 500 for large US stocks. Other investments may need a more suitable comparison.

Compare the same dates.

A one-year stock result must be compared with a one-year market result.

What the comparison cannot prove

Beating the market does not prove that a company is safe, profitable, affordable, or suitable for you.

Direction and comparison are separate
↑↑Stock rises moreOutperforming
↑Stock rises lessUp, but underperforming
↓↓Stock falls moreWeak relative result

How to read it: first ask whether the stock rose or fell. Then ask whether it did better or worse than the benchmark over exactly the same dates.

How PlainStock uses it

The wider-market comparison contributes 20% of the score. Treat it as one clue, not a final answer.
