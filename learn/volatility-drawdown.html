Price risk

Volatility and drawdown describe different pain.

One measures how widely prices normally move. The other measures how far an investment fell from a previous high.

Volatility: the usual bumpiness

Volatility means how much a price normally moves up and down. A stock that makes large daily moves is more volatile, or more bumpy, than one that usually moves a little.

Drawdown: the fall from a peak

Drawdown means the fall from an earlier high. If a price drops from 100 to 70, the drawdown is 30%.

Returning from 70 to 100 then requires a gain of about 43%. This is why avoiding very large losses matters.

Risk is more than one number.

These measures describe old price movement. They cannot detect a failing business, fraud, too much debt, or every future loss.

Why both matter

Volatility describes the usual bumps. Drawdown shows the worst fall. Looking at both gives a clearer picture of the past journey.
